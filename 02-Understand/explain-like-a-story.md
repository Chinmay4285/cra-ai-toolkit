# Explain Like a Story: Narrative Learning

## PURPOSE
Understand clinical research concepts through narrative stories that make ideas memorable and show real-world consequences. Story-based learning often sticks better than factual explanations.

## WHEN TO USE
When you want to deeply understand WHY something matters, or when you learn better through narratives and examples.

## WHAT TO PROVIDE
- Your study context
- Your protocol
- The concept/procedure/requirement you want to understand

## EXPECTED OUTPUT
A narrative explanation with characters, consequences, and CRA implications. Followed by \"The 5 Things to Remember\" and \"What a CRA Should Watch For.\"

---

# EXPLAIN LIKE A STORY PROMPT

```
COPY FROM HERE
========================================================================================

You are a clinical research storyteller explaining a concept through narrative.

STORY STRUCTURE:

1. SET THE SCENE
   Create a realistic scenario where this concept matters:
   - A specific patient or investigator
   - A specific study site or situation
   - A concrete moment where this concept applies
   - Build context that makes the listener care

2. INTRODUCE THE CHALLENGE
   What problem or question does the character face?
   Why does it matter?
   What could go wrong if this concept is misunderstood?

3. TELL THE STORY
   Walk through what happens chronologically:
   - Patient enrolls and meets [concept]
   - What does this mean for the patient?
   - What does this mean for the site/investigator?
   - What could go wrong?
   - How does understanding this concept help?

4. SHOW THE CONSEQUENCES
   What happens if the concept is applied correctly?
   What happens if it's ignored or misunderstood?
   Make the stakes clear

5. CONNECT TO THE STUDY
   How does this concept apply to THIS study specifically?
   What would happen in this study if [concept] wasn't understood?
   What should the CRA watch for?

6. END WITH THE LESSON
   What's the key takeaway?
   Why does this concept matter?
   How will the CRA use this understanding?

7. "THE 5 THINGS TO REMEMBER"
   Five bullet points summarizing key lessons

8. "WHAT A CRA SHOULD WATCH FOR"
   Red flags and signs that this concept is being misapplied
   Questions to ask sites
   Procedures to verify

SOURCE GROUNDING:
- Base the story on actual protocol details
- Reference specific study requirements
- Make the story realistic to this type of study

TONE:
- Engaging and memorable
- Professional but not dry
- Show why this matters
- Make the listener care about understanding this

========================================================================================
END PROMPT
```

## EXAMPLE STORY TOPICS

**"Tell me the story of the washout period in this study"**
- Introduce a patient with lingering drug effects
- Show why washout matters for clean baseline
- Show what happens if washout is skipped
- End with CRA's role in ensuring compliance

**"Tell me the story of why we have tight visit windows"**
- Patient arrives late
- Show impact on assessments
- Show impact on data integrity
- Show why windows exist

**"Tell me the story of eligibility criteria"**
- Fictional patient borderline on criteria
- Show what happens if enrolled despite questionable eligibility
- Show investigator/site perspective
- Show how CRA role in verification

**"Tell me the story of this endpoint"**
- Patient completing the primary assessment
- Why we measure it this way
- What could go wrong in measurement
- What makes good data vs. bad data

## TIPS FOR LEARNING FROM STORIES

1. **Picture the story**
   - Visualize the patient, site, investigator
   - See yourself in the CRA role
   - Imagine how you'd handle it

2. **Identify the lesson**
   - What's the core principle?
   - How does it apply beyond this study?
   - When will you encounter this in real work?

3. **Make it personal**
   - Imagine this happening at YOUR site
   - What would you do?
   - How would you verify/handle it?

4. **Remember the consequences**
   - Stories are memorable because they show stakes
   - This makes the concept stick better
   - You'll remember this during monitoring visits

---

## STORY VARIATIONS

**Different Perspectives:**
- "Tell me this from the patient's perspective"
- "Tell me this from the investigator's perspective"
- "Tell me this from the CRA's perspective"

**Different Outcomes:**
- "Tell me the story if this requirement wasn't met"
- "Tell me the story of what happens when sites get this right"
- "Tell me the story of a real mistake made on a site"

**Different Depth:**
- "Quick 2-minute story version"
- "Detailed story with all the nuances"
- "Multiple related stories that show different angles"
