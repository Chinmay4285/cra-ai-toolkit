# Build Study Knowledge: Structured Study Onboarding

## PURPOSE
Guide yourself through a structured, progressive onboarding to deeply understand a clinical study. This prompt helps you work WITH the Study Brain to systematically learn every essential aspect of your study.

## WHEN TO USE
Use this after you've loaded the Study Brain prompt. This guides HOW to use the Study Brain most effectively for learning. Think of it as a learning curriculum/roadmap.

Perfect for:
- First day with a new study
- Preparing yourself to be study-ready
- Ensuring you don't miss critical concepts
- Building knowledge progressively from basic to complex

## WHAT TO PROVIDE
- Your already-loaded Study Brain conversation (use the same one)
- Your available time (15 minutes, 1 hour, full day)

## EXPECTED OUTPUT
A structured learning pathway with specific questions to ask Claude and checkpoints to verify understanding.

---

# BUILD STUDY KNOWLEDGE PROMPT

## Your Study Learning Curriculum

This is a structured pathway to master your study. Work through these phases in order.

### PHASE 1: Study Fundamentals (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand what this study is at a high level.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "In 2-3 sentences, what is this study trying to accomplish?"
2. "Who are the patients in this study and why do they need this treatment?"
3. "What are the main treatment options and how are they different?"
4. "How long will patients be in the study?"
5. "What is the primary thing we're measuring to know if it works?"

**Checkpoint:** You can explain the study in 1 minute to someone unfamiliar with it.

---

### PHASE 2: Patient Journey (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand what happens to a patient from start to finish.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "Walk me through a patient's journey from screening to final visit. What happens at each step?"
2. "Create a timeline diagram showing all the visits and what happens at each one"
3. "What assessment are we doing at each visit? Why does each assessment matter?"
4. "What happens between visits? Are patients taking the study medication at home?"
5. "What could cause a patient to leave the study early?"

**Checkpoint:** You can describe a patient's full journey through the study without referencing materials.

---

### PHASE 3: Eligibility (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Know exactly who can and cannot be in this study.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "Create flashcards for the key inclusion criteria"
2. "Create flashcards for the key exclusion criteria"
3. "Which eligibility criteria are most commonly misunderstood?"
4. "If a patient has [common scenario], can they still be in the study? Why or why not?"
5. "What would disqualify a patient at screening vs. later in the study?"

**Checkpoint:** Quiz yourself on eligibility using the flashcards. Score 100% before moving on.

---

### PHASE 4: Endpoints & Assessments (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand what we're measuring and why.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "What is the primary endpoint and how is it measured? Why this endpoint?"
2. "What are the secondary endpoints?"
3. "For each endpoint, who does the measurement? When? How?"
4. "What could go wrong with measuring this endpoint?"
5. "Create a diagram showing when each assessment occurs and what it measures"

**Checkpoint:** Explain what would happen if an endpoint assessment was missed at a key timepoint.

---

### PHASE 5: Safety Monitoring (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Know how patient safety is protected.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "What safety assessments are done? How often?"
2. "What labs are drawn, and what would be concerning values?"
3. "What adverse events require immediate reporting vs. routine reporting?"
4. "Who do I contact if a patient has a serious safety issue?"
5. "Create a flowchart showing safety decision-making"

**Checkpoint:** You know the difference between reportable and non-reportable adverse events for your study.

---

### PHASE 6: Your CRA Responsibilities (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Know exactly what YOU need to do.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "What are my key responsibilities as CRA on this study?"
2. "What must I verify at each visit?"
3. "What source documents must I check?"
4. "What data quality issues should I watch for?"
5. "What decisions do I escalate vs. handle myself?"
6. "Create a monitoring visit checklist for each visit type"

**Checkpoint:** Walk through a hypothetical visit and explain what you'd check and verify.

---

### PHASE 7: Critical Study Facts (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Know the numbers and details you'll reference constantly.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "Create a study fact sheet with: visit windows, enrollment target, treatment duration, key numbers"
2. "What are the most important study-specific terms/acronyms I need to know?"
3. "What numbers appear repeatedly in the protocol?"
4. "Quiz me on critical study numbers"

**Checkpoint:** Create a one-page quick reference you can keep at your desk.

---

### PHASE 8: Protocol Deviations & Risks (15 minutes)

**Goal:** Understand common problems and how to handle them.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "What are common protocol deviations on this type of study?"
2. "What could cause a patient to have a missed visit? What do I do?"
3. "What would be a critical deviation vs. minor deviation?"
4. "If a patient comes to visit 3 days outside the window, what's the impact?"
5. "Find potential risk areas in this study"

**Checkpoint:** You can identify what's a deviation vs. what's acceptable.

---

### PHASE 9: Connected Concepts (15 minutes)

**Goal:** See how all requirements work together.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "Why does [requirement 1] connect to [requirement 2]?"
2. "If we didn't do [procedure], what else would be affected?"
3. "Show me how the visit schedule, inclusion criteria, and endpoints work together"
4. "What would happen if we changed [this parameter]?"

**Checkpoint:** Explain the study as an integrated system, not isolated requirements.

---

### PHASE 10: Final Integration Quiz (20 minutes)

**Goal:** Test comprehensive understanding.

Ask your Study Brain:

1. "Give me 20 tough questions about this study - mixture of memorization, application, and judgment"
2. "For any I miss, explain why that's important"
3. "Based on my answers, what should I study more?"

**Checkpoint:** Score 85%+. If lower, review weak areas.

---

## Timeline Options

**For Different Time Investments:**

**If you have 2 hours:**
- Do Phases 1-7 (Fundamentals through Critical Facts)
- Come back to Phases 8-10 the next day

**If you have 4 hours:**
- Do all 10 phases
- Perfect for first day onboarding

**If you have 1 hour:**
- Do Phases 1, 2, 3, 6
- Covers most critical knowledge
- Do other phases over next few days

**If you have 30 minutes:**
- Do Phases 1, 2, 6
- Bare minimum to understand what you're doing
- Do all phases when you have time

---

## Before Your First Monitoring Visit

**Complete at least:**
- Phases 1-7
- Phase 8 (protocol deviations)
- Understand Phase 9 connections

**Before you leave for the site:**
- Review your monitoring checklist from Phase 6
- Quiz yourself on Phase 7 facts
- Run through a scenario in Phase 8

---

## Continuous Learning

**Weekly:** Pick a topic and ask `/teach me [topic]` in your Study Brain

**Before visits:** Ask `/visit prep for [visit name]`

**When confused:** Ask `/find gaps in [section]` to find what you don't understand

**Ongoing:** Every few weeks, take another full quiz to check retention

---

## Key Principle

You're not just memorizing facts—you're building an integrated understanding of how your study works.

After completing all 10 phases, you should be able to:
- ✅ Explain the study to a new CRA
- ✅ Understand why each requirement exists
- ✅ Predict what problems might occur
- ✅ Handle problems when they arise
- ✅ Make good CRA decisions
- ✅ Pass a comprehensive study knowledge assessment

This is mastery, not just knowledge.
