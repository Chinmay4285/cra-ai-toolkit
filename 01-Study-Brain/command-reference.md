# Study Brain Command Reference

## QUICK COMMANDS FOR YOUR STUDY BRAIN CONVERSATION

Once your Study Brain is loaded with your protocol and study context, use these short commands to trigger specific types of output.

**In your Study Brain conversation, simply type the command and follow it with what you want analyzed.**

---

## LEARNING & TEACHING COMMANDS

### /teach me about [topic]
Ask Claude to explain a topic clearly with study-specific examples.

*Example:* "/teach me about the washout period"

*Output:* Clear explanation of what it is, why it's required, and what it means for CRA work.

---

### /story about [concept]
Ask for a narrative explanation of a concept with characters, consequences, and CRA implications.

*Example:* "/story about why we're measuring the primary endpoint"

*Output:* Story format that makes the concept memorable and shows why it matters.

---

### /explain for [audience]
Tailor explanation for a specific audience.

*Example:* "/explain for a site coordinator what source data verification means"

*Output:* Explanation calibrated for that person's perspective.

---

### /simple explanation of [concept]
Get the most simplified version possible.

*Example:* "/simple explanation of randomization"

*Output:* One or two sentences capturing the essential idea.

---

### /deep dive into [topic]
Go beyond the basics; explore nuance, reasoning, and connected concepts.

*Example:* "/deep dive into why the visit window is ±3 days instead of ±7"

*Output:* Detailed explanation of clinical, operational, and statistical reasoning.

---

## VISUAL COMMANDS

### /diagram of [process/concept]
Create a visual diagram (flowchart, timeline, decision tree, etc.) explaining something.

*Example:* "/diagram of the patient journey through this study"

*Output:* ASCII or Mermaid diagram showing the flow.

---

### /timeline of [element]
Create a timeline visualization.

*Example:* "/timeline of all study assessments across visits"

*Output:* Visual timeline showing when things happen.

---

### /table comparing [items]
Organize information in table format for easy comparison.

*Example:* "/table comparing inclusion and exclusion criteria"

*Output:* Comparison table with clear organization.

---

## MEMORIZATION COMMANDS

### /quiz me on [topic]
Generate study-specific quiz questions.

*Example:* "/quiz me on eligibility criteria"

*Output:* Series of questions (you answer; Claude grades and explains).

---

### /flashcards for [topic]
Generate flashcard-style Q&A pairs.

*Example:* "/flashcards for study procedures"

*Output:* Quizlet-compatible format you can import.

---

### /scenario quiz
Present realistic situations and ask what you'd do.

*Example:* "/scenario quiz about deviations"

*Output:* Series of realistic scenarios; Claude evaluates your responses.

---

### /rapid fire review
Quick-fire facts about the study.

*Example:* "/rapid fire review - give me the 2-minute study facts"

*Output:* Essential facts delivered quickly.

---

## ANALYSIS COMMANDS

### /compare [item 1] vs [item 2]
Show differences and implications.

*Example:* "/compare primary endpoint vs secondary endpoint"

*Output:* Clear comparison showing what's different and why it matters.

---

### /find risks in [section]
Identify potential safety, compliance, or data quality risks.

*Example:* "/find risks in the inclusion criteria"

*Output:* Listed risks with explanations and implications.

---

### /find gaps in [section]
Identify missing or ambiguous information.

*Example:* "/find gaps in the monitoring section"

*Output:* Listed gaps with suggestions for verification.

---

### /find inconsistencies
Identify conflicts or contradictions between sections or documents.

*Example:* "/find inconsistencies between the protocol and ICF"

*Output:* Listed inconsistencies with analysis.

---

### /what if [scenario]
Analyze the impact of a hypothetical situation.

*Example:* "/what if a patient missed their week 4 visit"

*Output:* Analysis of implications and how to handle it.

---

## MONITORING VISIT COMMANDS

### /visit prep for [visit name/timepoint]
Prepare for a specific monitoring visit.

*Example:* "/visit prep for baseline visit"

*Output:* Comprehensive preparation checklist including what to verify, questions to ask, documents to review.

---

### /visit followup
After a monitoring visit, get organized follow-up tasks.

*Example (in conversation):* "Here's what happened at my visit. [Details]. /visit followup"

*Output:* Organized action items, follow-ups, documentation needs.

---

### /common visit issues
Learn what typically goes wrong at monitoring visits.

*Example:* "/common visit issues"

*Output:* List of frequent problems with how to prevent/handle them.

---

## WORKFLOW COMMANDS

### /questions for the site
Generate intelligent questions to ask during site interactions.

*Example:* "/questions for the site about enrollment challenges"

*Output:* Structured list of smart questions with follow-ups.

---

### /deviation analysis
Analyze whether something is a protocol deviation.

*Example (in conversation):* "Patient arrived on day 18 instead of day 14±3. /deviation analysis"

*Output:* Analysis of whether it's a deviation and severity level.

---

### /action items
Summarize open action items and next steps.

*Example:* "/action items from last visit"

*Output:* Organized list with priorities and deadlines.

---

## ASSESSMENT COMMANDS

### /knowledge check on [topic]
Check your understanding of a topic.

*Example:* "/knowledge check on the study design"

*Output:* Series of questions gauging your understanding; identifies gaps.

---

### /my weak areas
Identify topics where your understanding is weakest.

*Example (after quizzes):* "/my weak areas"

*Output:* Analysis of what you've struggled with; recommendations for focused study.

---

### /study readiness check
Overall assessment of whether you're ready for real monitoring work.

*Example:* "/study readiness check"

*Output:* Evaluation of knowledge across key areas; recommendations for additional prep.

---

## SYNTHESIS COMMANDS

### /connect [concept 1] to [concept 2]
Show how two concepts relate.

*Example:* "/connect the eligibility criteria to the inclusion population"

*Output:* Explanation of how they're related and why both matter.

---

### /summary of [section]
Create a concise summary of a protocol section.

*Example:* "/summary of visit schedule"

*Output:* Condensed version capturing essential facts.

---

### /one-page summary of [aspect]
Ultra-condensed summary.

*Example:* "/one-page summary of everything I need to know before a monitoring visit"

*Output:* Single-page summary you can print and take with you.

---

## ADVANCED COMMANDS

### /assume I'm [role] - [question]
Shift perspective to another role.

*Example:* "/assume I'm the site coordinator - what are their biggest challenges with this study?"

*Output:* Answer from that role's perspective.

---

### /critical thinking on [topic]
Challenge your thinking; ask probing questions.

*Example:* "/critical thinking on why we exclude patients over 65"

*Output:* Questions that make you think deeply rather than just memorize.

---

### /red flags
Identify potential concerns or warning signs.

*Example:* "/red flags in site reporting"

*Output:* Potential concerns to watch for.

---

## TIPS FOR USING COMMANDS

1. **Be specific:** "/quiz me on week 4 visit" not "/quiz me"

2. **Combine commands:** "/diagram of [process] then quiz me on it"

3. **Add context:** "/teach me about adverse events - I'm new to clinical research"

4. **Chain commands:** Ask one command, get output, use follow-up command

5. **Modify output:** "Give me that as a table" or "Make that shorter"

---

## If a Command Doesn't Work

Your Study Brain understands these types of requests. If a specific command doesn't work:

- Rephrase it more clearly
- Add more context
- Use the underlying concept instead (e.g., instead of "/find-risks," say "What could go wrong with the eligibility assessment?")

Claude will generally understand what you're trying to do even if the command syntax isn't perfect.
